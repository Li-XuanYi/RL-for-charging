\begin{abstract}
\newrev{Charging balancing control of lithium-ion batteries must shorten charging time while maintaining state-of-charge (SOC) consistency among cells. Traditional methods typically combine a balancing algorithm with a dedicated equalization topology, which increases system complexity and may dissipate or transfer energy from high-SOC cells. This paper proposes a multi-balancing-agent reinforcement learning (MBA-RL) framework in which each agent selects the charging current of one cell while coordinating pack-level SOC balancing. The framework does not require a dedicated equalization topology and applies a model-based safety screen before the requested currents are executed. Over 60 evaluation runs covering 12 unseen initial-SOC conditions and five independent training runs, MBA-RL reduces accumulated SOC imbalance, defined as $\int\sigma(t)\,\mathrm{d}t$, by 12.65\% and 10.82\% relative to shared independent Q-learning and centralized deep Q-learning under the same discrete action setting. The safety-constrained execution mechanism satisfies the sampled limits in all 52 safety tests and, when included during training, enables all tests to reach the charging objective. These results demonstrate coordinated cell-level charging and pack-level balancing under the tested conditions.}
\end{abstract}
