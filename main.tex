\begin{abstract}
Lithium-ion batteries are widely used in electric vehicles and energy storage systems because of their high energy density, long cycle life, and low self-discharge rate. However, differences among cells in a battery pack can reduce usable capacity, charging efficiency, and safety. Therefore, achieving fast charging while maintaining state of charge (SOC) balance is important for battery pack operation.
This paper proposes a multi-balancing-agent reinforcement learning (MBA-RL) framework for coordinated charging control without a dedicated equalization topology. In MBA-RL, each agent regulates the charging current of one cell, while a shared objective coordinates charging progress and SOC balancing across the battery pack. A model-based safety mechanism evaluates the requested currents against voltage, temperature, and SOC limits before execution. The framework restricts the charging currents to nonnegative values, thereby avoiding the discharge of cells with high SOC.
In 60 evaluations covering 12 unseen initial SOC conditions across five independent training runs, MBA-RL reduces accumulated SOC imbalance by 12.65\% and 10.82\% relative to shared independent Q-learning and centralized deep Q-learning, respectively. The safety mechanism satisfies the sampled constraints in all 52 safety tests. When the same mechanism is included during training, all 52 tests also reach the charging objective.
\end{abstract}
